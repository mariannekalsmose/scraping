Hiroshi Amano (天野 浩, Amano Hiroshi; born September 11, 1960) is a Japanese electrical engineer specializing in the field of semiconductor technology. He is a co-inventor of the blue LED, for which he was awarded the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physics along with Isamu Akasaki and Shuji Nakamura.[1]

Biography

[edit]

Hiroshi Amano was born on September 11, 1960, in Hamamatsu, Shizuoka Prefecture, Japan, the son of Yoshiko and Tatsuji Amano.[2]

In 1979, Amano entered Nagoya University as a student of the Department of Electrical Engineering,[2] graduating with a Bachelor of Engineering in 1983. He then went to the Graduate School of Engineering, where he received a Master of Engineering in 1985 and a Doctor of Engineering in 1989.[3][4]

In 1988, Amano became Research Associate in the School of Engineering at Nagoya University. From 1992 to 2010, he was Assistant Professor, Associate Professor, and Professor in the Faculty of Science and Technology at Meijo University. In 2010, he returned to Nagoya University, where he is currently a professor in the Graduate School of Engineering.[4] Amano has been Director of the Akasaki Research Center at Nagoya since 2011.[2]

Research

[edit]

Amano joined Professor Isamu Akasaki's group in 1982 as an undergraduate student. Since then, he has been doing research on the growth, characterization, and device applications of group-III-nitride semiconductors, which are well known as materials used in blue light-emitting diodes (LEDs) today. In 1985, he developed low-temperature deposited buffer layers for the growth of group-III-nitride semiconductor films on a sapphire substrate, which led to the realization of group-III-nitride semiconductor-based LEDs and laser diodes. In 1989, he succeeded in growing p-type GaN and fabricating a p-n-junction-type GaN-based UV/blue LED for the first time in the world.[2]

Known to be keen on research, Amano's laboratory was always lit late at night, such as weekdays, holidays, New Year's Day, and was called "no night castle".[5] According to his students in the laboratory, Amano has an optimistic and temperate personality, and is never angry.[6][7]

Family

[edit]

Amano's wife was a Japanese lecturer at Comenius University in Bratislava, Slovakia.[8]
